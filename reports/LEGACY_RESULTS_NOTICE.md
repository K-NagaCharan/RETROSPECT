**Effective Date:** 2026-10-19  
**Status:** **LEGACY DIAGNOSTIC — NOT EVIDENCE**  
**Applies To:** All prior benchmark reports, CSV summaries, raw outputs, and metrics derived from the Category A pilot ($N=5$) and Category B provisional dataset ($N=29$) evaluated at commit `17ea399217737f8d5fe2c32c310b69c2f812b84d`.

## 1. Formal Notice of Status

All historical evaluation results previously reported for Retrospect configurations **B1 (Structural Only)**, **B2 (Historical Only)**, **B3 (Combined Linear)**, and **B4 (Combined RRF-v2)** are hereby designated as **Historical Diagnostics Only**.

Under no circumstances should these legacy numbers be cited as confirmatory scientific evidence, published as benchmark claims, or used to assert the superiority of any retrieval configuration over another.

---

## 2. Documented Methodological & Data Defects in Legacy Benchmark

Thorough bounded evidence audits completed on 2026-10-19 revealed that the legacy benchmark datasets contain systemic defects:

1. **Invalid or Misclassified Historical Relationships:**
   - Legacy cases generated by automated heuristic mining (RefactoringMiner oracle dumps) contain unverified refactoring classifications that lack human domain review.
   - Target methods in several cases represent disparate implementations rather than true functional continuations of historical predecessors.
2. **Incorrect or Insufficient Target Spans:**
   - Several cases defined single-line or incomplete target declaration spans, omitting method bodies and parameter bounds required for precise AST containment.
3. **Potential Query Leakage from Identifiers:**
   - A substantial proportion of queries in the legacy dataset explicitly embed the historical or current method identifier (e.g. `"Locate current implementation corresponding to historical method <name>"`), introducing synthetic token overlap that artificially inflates lexical and structural retrieval scores.
4. **Statistical Dependence Across Origin-Commit Clusters:**
   - Multiple cases share identical `origin_commit` hashes and repository contexts (e.g. multiple getters modified in a single commit). The legacy reporting treated these as independent statistical observations, violating independence assumptions and distorting metric variances.
5. **Structural Score Inflation via Identifier Leakage:**
   - Because queries explicitly contained the predecessor identifier, and predecessor identifiers frequently share token roots with the successor (or exist in adjacent class names), B1 structural retrieval scores were artificially boosted on several cases.
6. **Conflation of AST/Compilation Validity with Semantic Lineage:**
   - Earlier validation passes treated successful code parsing, compilation, or commit existence in Git as proof of historical lineage. Syntactic validity at snapshot $T$ provides zero proof of causal code derivation.
7. **Lookback Horizon Artifacts:**
   - The rigid 50-commit historical lookback horizon caused an artificial 62%–80% abstention rate because 20 of 29 Category B cases had true refactorings lying between 52 and 637 commits in the past.

## 3. Policy on Citing Legacy Metrics & Hypotheses

- **Descriptive Use Only:** The reported numbers in `RETROSPECT_EXPERIMENTAL_RESULTS.md` and `RETROSPECT_DIAGNOSTIC_FAILURE_AUDIT_AND_DECISION.md` may be cited **solely** to describe the runtime behavior, bottlenecks, and engineering evolution of the earlier diagnostic pipeline.
- **No Superiority Claims:** No claim that B4 outperforms B1, B2, or B3 is supported by the legacy evidence. In fact, on clean Category A pilot cases, B1 achieved higher Recall@10 (60.0%) than B4 (40.0%).
- **Fusion-Dilution Status:** The hypothesis that linear score fusion (B3) causes destructive rank dilution when one channel abstains, and that RRF (B4) mitigates this via zero-signal gating, remains an **unconfirmed working hypothesis**. It must be empirically validated on the rebuilt, Git-first benchmark rather than treated as proven fact.
